# Working capital vs. term loans: which fits your business?

When small business owners start looking for financing, two options come up more than any others: working capital loans and term loans. Both put money in your hands, but they're built for different situations. Understanding the difference before you apply can save you time and help you land financing that actually fits how your business operates.

## What working capital financing is for

Working capital is the money that keeps your business running day to day — payroll, rent, inventory, supplies, and the dozens of other expenses that hit before customers pay. When there's a gap between what's going out and what's coming in, working capital financing bridges it.

This category includes short-term loans, business lines of credit, merchant cash advances, and invoice factoring. What these products share is speed and flexibility. They're built for businesses that need access to capital quickly and may only need it for a few months at a time.

A restaurant that needs to stock up before a busy season. A contractor waiting on a client who's slow to pay. A retailer that has to buy inventory before the holidays. These are working capital situations — temporary cash flow gaps, not long-term capital investments.

Repayment terms tend to be shorter, often six to eighteen months. The application process is usually lighter on documentation. And if you use a business line of credit, you can draw what you need, pay it down, and draw again — only paying for what you actually use.

## What term loans are for

A term loan gives you a lump sum upfront that you repay on a fixed schedule over a defined period — often two to five years, sometimes longer. The payment amount, the rate, and the timeline are all set from the beginning, which makes budgeting much more predictable.

Term loans are the right tool when you have a specific, one-time investment in mind. Buying equipment. Opening a second location. Hiring and training a new team. Refinancing higher-cost debt. These are capital decisions with a clear purpose and a payback horizon that extends over years, not months.

Because term loans involve a longer commitment, lenders look at your business more carefully. Time in business, annual revenue, credit history, and how you plan to use the funds all factor in. The documentation is more thorough, and the decision process can take longer.

## Questions worth asking yourself before you apply

Is this a recurring cash flow issue, or a one-time capital need? If money gets tight regularly — every slow season, every time a big invoice is outstanding — that points toward working capital tools or a revolving line of credit. If you're funding something specific and finite, a term loan makes more sense.

How long will you need the money? Short-term gaps call for short-term financing. Long-term investments should be financed on longer terms so the payment doesn't strain your cash flow.

How consistent is your revenue? If your income is seasonal or unpredictable, a fixed monthly payment on a term loan can create pressure during slow periods. Flexible repayment structures may be a better match.

Do you need flexibility or certainty? Lines of credit give you flexibility to use what you need. Term loans give you a predictable structure. Many businesses use both at different times for different purposes — that's common and makes good sense.
